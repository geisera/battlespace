# Starship Tactical Combat Rules

## Sequence of Play
1. Initiative Phase
2. Allocation Phase
3. Action Phase 1
4. Action Phase 2
5. Reaction Phase

Each combat round is resolved in five phases.

### 1. Initiative Phase

**Each ship makes an Initiative Test:**

2d6 + Command Rating + Initiative modifiers

Record each ship’s Initiative Score from highest to lowest.

In Action Phase 1, ships act from highest Initiative to lowest.
In Action Phase 2, ships act from lowest Initiative to highest.

This means a ship that acts early in the first Action Phase acts late in the second.
***Note: Consider additional initiative check for action phase 2***

Resolve tied Initiative Scores by comparing Command Rating. If still tied, reroll between the tied ships.

---

### 2. Allocation Phase

Each ship distributes Power equal to its current Reactor Output among:

Propulsion
Shield emitters 1–6
Weapons
Reserve

Allocation is simultaneous and hidden. All players reveal their allocations after every ship has committed.

Power remains assigned until the next Allocation Phase unless rerouted by a rule or Command Point ability.

The total Power allocated cannot exceed the ship’s current Reactor Output.

**Propulsion**

Propulsion Power is spent to perform Maneuver actions and movement reactions.

Each Maneuver action normally costs 1 Propulsion Power.

Maneuver actions include ship movement to an adjacent hex. And, turning the ship to a new heading.

**Shields**

Power is assigned separately to the ship’s six shield emitter facings.

A shield emitter must receive at least 1 Power to operate and regenerate normally.

Additional Power may improve Shield Rating, Shield Points, or Regeneration according to the shield system’s profile.

**Weapons**

Each weapon or weapon group must receive enough Power to fire.

Power is spent when the weapon attacks. Spent Weapon Power cannot be used again during the same round.

**Reserve**

Reserve Power is not assigned to a specific system.

It may be used for reactions, emergency reinforcement, or power rerouting when permitted by a rule.

### 3. Action Phase 1

Ships activate from highest Initiative to lowest.

When activated, a ship chooses one of the following:

- Maneuver
- Attack
- Minor Action

Resolve the chosen action completely before the next ship activates.

Damage and other effects take effect immediately.

### 4. Action Phase 2

Ships activate from lowest Initiative to highest.

Each ship again chooses one of the following:

- Maneuver
- Attack
- Minor Action

A ship may choose the same action type in both Action Phases, provided it can pay all required power costs.

For example, a ship may:

Maneuver twice
Attack twice with different powered weapons
Attack twice with the same weapon if that weapon can fire more than once
Maneuver and then Attack
Attack and then Maneuver
Perform up to four Minor Actions

### Shields
A ship’s defensive shield is divided into six facings arranged around its hex base. Each facing protects one 60-degree arc.

**Determining the Shield Facing Hit**

Draw a line from the attacker’s hex to the target’s hex. The side of the target’s hex crossed by that line determines which shield facing is struck.

An attack from directly ahead strikes the Fore shield. An attack from behind strikes the Aft shield. Attacks arriving along either side strike one of the four angled shield facings.

If the line passes exactly through a corner between two shield facings, the defender chooses which of the two adjacent facings is struck.

**When an attack hits a shielded ship:**

1. Determine the shield facing struck.
2. Subtract that facing’s Shield Rating from the attack’s Damage.
3. Apply the remaining damage to the facing’s current Shield Points.
4. If its Shield Points reach 0, that facing collapses.
5. Apply any excess damage to the armor or module behind the same shield.

Shield Rating cannot reduce damage below 0.

**Shield Collapse**

A shield facing collapses when its current Shield Points reach 0.

A collapsed facing:

- Provides no Shield Rating.
- Provides no Shield Points.
- Allows attacks to strike the corresponding armor directly.

The other five shield facings remain operational.

A collapsed facing becomes active again as soon as it regenerates at least 1 Shield Point.



Damage done by an attack is always deducted from the shield first.
Excessive damage to a shield emitter will disable that emitter until it is repaired.

### Weapons
Weapons may be charged during the combat phase. Or, before during the allocation phase. 
If charged during the allocation phase, captains will receive a damage bonus. But, they may be detected by enemy sensors.

#### Beam Weapons
Beam weapons may be charged from 1 point, up to its maximum. Beam weapon damage is diminished by range, and by terrain effects. But, are highly effective against shields.
Beam weapons always hit.
Beam weapons may be used to target incoming missiles if they are already charged.

#### Missile Weapons
Missiles require only 1 point to fire, and do damage equal to their multiplier and die roll.
Ships carry a finite number of missiles.
Charged missiles must be fired or jettisoned.
Missiles may not target specific ship's systems. Hit locations are random.
Missiles may be configured for specific purposes: armor-piercing, EMP, etc.
Missile attacks require a to-hit calculation.

## Action
The captain may execute, at his discretion, any of the following actions for which he has allocated power in any order.

### Electronic warfare
- Cloaking
- Jamming
- Hacking
- Target lock
- Deep scan

### Maneuver
The captain may move his ship a number of spaces equal to the maneuver points assigned during allocation.
More massive ships require more points to move, and to turn.
